# This video's own bar

Every critic applies these on top of its skill.

- **Fair to the other side.** It argues a position, so each counterargument it raises (Fowler on determinism, Dijkstra, the three
  studies) is stated in its strongest honest form, never as a straw man, and the video does not claim the history proves more than
  it shows. A line that overstates the thesis is an accuracy finding.
- **Opinion is said as opinion.** The lines `sources.md` marks **opinion** are the narrator's view, in his first person or as a
  question; a viewer must not take one as a cited fact.
- **Every number carries its population on screen**: who was measured, how many, when, and who measured it.
- **Quotes are exact.** A quote on screen matches `sources.md` word for word; a paraphrase is never in quotation marks.
- **A meme lands a point the narration already made**, it never carries a claim of its own, and its text is readable in the time
  it is up.
- **Kind.** The jokes target the argument, never a person; no meme puts a real person's name on the joke.
- **For YouTube.** A developer who pressed play on the title "It was never about the code" knows within ten seconds what the video
  will argue, and the ending gives them the question to ask.
