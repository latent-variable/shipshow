In 1952, Grace Hopper had what she called a compiler. In her words, she had a running compiler and nobody would touch it. They told her computers could only do arithmetic. Around 1954, John Backus started on another compiler at IBM, FORTRAN. Programmers didn't believe a machine could write programs as efficient as theirs. Backus later said many of them saw themselves as a priesthood, guarding skills too complex for ordinary mortals. Don't trust code you didn't write by hand. Sound familiar?
