In 1952, Grace Hopper had a program that wrote programs. She called it a compiler. In her words, she had a running compiler and nobody would touch it. They told her computers could only do arithmetic. Two years later, John Backus and his team at IBM started on FORTRAN. Programmers didn't buy that either. Backus wrote that their claims of efficient programs were disbelieved. He later called those programmers a priesthood, guarding skills too complex for ordinary mortals.
