In 1952, Grace Hopper had a program that wrote programs. She called it a compiler. In her words, she had a running compiler and nobody would touch it. They told her computers could only do arithmetic. Two years later, John Backus started on FORTRAN at IBM. Programmers didn't believe a machine could write programs as efficient as theirs. Backus later called them a priesthood, guarding skills too complex for ordinary mortals. Don't trust code you didn't write by hand. Sound familiar?
