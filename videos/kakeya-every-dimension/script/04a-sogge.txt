[clear, explaining, with quiet confidence, then a dry aside] In 1991, Christopher Sogge asked how bad a focus can get, on average. At its worst instant, a wave can be enormously concentrated. But a focus can't last. A wave, it turns out, can't hold a pose. Average over a stretch of time, and the wave is always tamer than its worst moment.

[clear, careful, planting a number for later] Sogge guessed exactly how much tamer, allowing only an arbitrarily small loss. His guess is called the local smoothing conjecture. And one power, set by the dimension, is the critical case the whole problem turns on: four in two dimensions, three in three.

[quiet, pointed, planting it] Remember that number. It comes back.
