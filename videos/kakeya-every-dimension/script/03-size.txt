[clear, explaining a simple test, unhurried] Here's a test for size. Give every needle a little thickness, and watch how fast the area shrinks as the thickness does. A single line becomes a thin strip, and its area shrinks in step with the thickness: that's one dimensional. A filled square doesn't shrink at all: two dimensional. Shrink at a rate in between, and you're in between.

[building, then letting a striking number land] Now a Kakeya set. Make its needles a googol times thinner. A plain line that thin would cover a googolth of a unit. The Kakeya set still covers about a hundredth. It shrinks so slowly that, by this test, it's two dimensional: the full size of the plane. The Kakeya conjecture says the same in every dimension. A set with a needle in every direction is always full size.
