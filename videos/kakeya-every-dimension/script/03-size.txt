[curious, posing a riddle to the viewer] So, a riddle. A Kakeya set can have no area at all. Is it basically nothing, a scribble of thin lines? Or is it, in some deeper sense, as solid as a filled square?

[clear, explaining why area can't answer] Area can't tell us. A single needle has no width, so it has no area either. By area, one lonely needle and a needle in every direction look the same: zero. So give the needles some width back. Take a real brush, and paint over every needle in the shape. Now there's paint to measure.

[clear, unhurried, showing the tool] Then do it again with a thinner brush. Here's the first brush. Then one half as wide. A quarter. An eighth. Each time, paint the whole shape again, and see how much paint it takes. How fast the paint shrinks tells you how big the shape really is.
