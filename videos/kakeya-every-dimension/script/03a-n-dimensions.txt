[clear, opening up a bigger space] And math doesn't stop at a flat table. In space, a point needs three numbers to say where it is. Fill a solid block with dabs, little cubes now, and halve the brush: twice as many across, down and deep. Eight times as many, two to the power three. Three dimensional. In four dimensional space, a point needs four numbers, even though nobody can picture it. Call that number n.

[clear, stating the conjecture] The Kakeya conjecture says that in n dimensional space, a set with a needle pointing every way is always full size: dimension n, like a solid block filling that space. The official version of the paint test is called Hausdorff dimension: same idea, stricter.
