# This video's own bar

Every critic applies these on top of its skill. The owner asked for a "really thorough", "very interesting, very fun, very
engaging" video, a breakdown for a general viewer that then goes into the details, for his channel, read by a narrator voice (his own voice is kept for his opinion videos, 2026-10-27). His bar
from his last channel video (videos/never-about-the-code/critics.md) carries over where it fits.

- **Right, first.** This is a complexity-theory result experts are still checking, and viewers will repeat what it says. A claim
  stronger than the paper or OpenAI's announcement makes is a finding at any size: the refutation is said as "if it holds", Lean
  is OpenAI's claim and checks a weaker bound than Theorem 1.1, and nothing in the video says P = NP, that SAT solvers got faster,
  or that a specific lower bound (edit distance) is now broken.
- **The core idea is understood, not name-dropped.** A developer who watched once can say why splitting into groups would help,
  what the dial does, and why the answer is read off zero from derivatives at easy points. A step that only names a technique is
  a finding.
- **ETH against P vs NP is unmistakable.** P ≠ NP rules out polynomial; ETH rules out the whole subexponential middle; ETH falling
  leaves P vs NP open. A frame or line that blurs these is a finding.
- **Fireship energy, funny first.** Punchy, fast, plain words, a joke where one fits. A stretch that sounds like a lecture or reads
  as written copy is a finding. Fast narration is wanted as long as each sentence can be followed.
- **Thoughts land together.** Each script line is one take; a join between lines must not cut a thought in half.
- **Math is readable.** Every formula on screen is large enough to read and stays up long enough to read while listening, and
  the spoken version matches it (the screen may be more exact, never different).
- **Every number carries its source on screen** where it is a measurement or a citation (the poll, the records, the theorem).
- **Shows the people** named for what they did, with a photo and a legible Commons credit where one is free (Cook, Impagliazzo,
  Williams); Paturi is a name card.
- **Memes land a point already made** and are readable in the time they are up.

- **Each act has its own feel.** The intro, the puzzle, the history, the house of cards, the paper landing, the heist, the
