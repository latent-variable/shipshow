[curious, inviting] So how do you count every setting without trying every one? Three ideas, stacked.

[clear, unhurried, explaining to a curious friend] First, a setup. Turn each rule into a tiny formula that gives one when the rule is happy, and zero when it isn't. For one setting, multiply all the rules together. You get one only if every rule is happy. Do that for every setting, add it all up, and you've counted the settings that work. But that's still two to the n settings to go through.

[clear, letting the numbers land] Idea one: split. Take sixty switches. That's two to the sixty settings, about a billion billion. Now suppose the switches fell into three separate groups of twenty, with no rule touching two groups. Each group has two to the twenty settings, about a million. Count each group, a million steps each. Every setting of one group pairs with every setting of the others, so the counts multiply. But finding them takes just a million steps per group: three million in all, instead of a billion billion.

[dry, understated] The catch: real problems don't split like that. Rules cross between groups everywhere.
