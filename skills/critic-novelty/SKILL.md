# Critic: novelty

**Judges:** whether a viewer who has seen this person's other videos could predict this one, scene by scene, and whether
each surprise is about the content. Not whether the persona keeps watching overall (`critic-engagement`), the speed and
stillness (`critic-pacing`), or whether a frame is well made (`critic-visuals`).

**Why it exists.** The owner, 2026-11-03: "I'm starting to see the patterns and it's becoming repetitive, and that
repetition and predictability don't help with engagement." A viewer who can predict the next beat stops attending to it
(habituation; Lang's limited-capacity work on cuts and the orienting response), and a feed full of creators racing the bar
makes boredom expensive. The other half is just as measured: an interesting detail that is NOT about the point lowers what
people understand and remember (the seductive-details effect, Harp and Mayer 1998; Sundararajan and Adesope 2020, a
meta-analysis). So the bar is surprise that teaches, never surprise instead of teaching. The research and the catalogue of
moves: `explain-concept`, *Break the formula*, and its `references/pattern-breaks.md`.

**Evidence:** the packet's *Devices* section (every editing move this video makes with how many of the person's other videos
made it, *staple* and *new to the channel*, the entrance shares, how each act opens, the rhythm of change), *Memes*,
*Moods*, *Stillness* and *Script, as voiced*; the frames; and the other videos themselves, read straight from their
folders (`videos/*/script/`, `scenes.html`, and `local/videos/` or `SHIPSHOW_VIDEOS` for another person's), so you can say
which earlier video already did a thing. Read the scripts of at least the two most recent other videos before you judge.

## Criteria

- **major:** every act opens the same way: the *Devices* act table shows one first piece and one entrance for every act
  (a chapter card fading in), or every lead is the same length. Vary it: one act cold-opens mid-sentence, one opens on a
  picture with no title, one on silence, one on a sound.
- **major:** the video's shape is the channel's formula unchanged. The formula as measured on 2026-11-03 across five
  videos: a startling question; a roadmap ("three questions", "three suspects", "here are the rules"); titled acts each
  opened by a card; a late "what does it mean / should you believe it" act; a close that calls back to the opening. A
  video for the channel breaks at least one of those on purpose, chosen for its subject (a cold open from the middle, the
  answer first and the mystery being how, an act told as a game or a timeline or a conversation, a fake ending).
- **major:** nothing new to the channel: no move in *Devices* marked *new to the channel*, no structure and no device any
  earlier video lacked. Every video adds at least one move the viewer has not seen here, chosen because the content
  calls for it.
- **major:** a minute or more of the video with no pattern break: a moment the viewer could not have predicted from the
  one before (a change of form, a scale jump, a hush, a callback, a broken expectation, a visual metaphor that literally
  happens, a punch-in on the word that matters). Name the stretch by its times.
- **major:** a surprise that does not carry the point: a gag, meme, sound or motion that is about nothing being said, or
  one that lands on the sentence introducing a new idea and competes with it. Wacky is welcome; random is a finding.
- **major:** three entrances make up over three quarters of the video's entrances (the *Devices* line), or one piece type
  carries most scenes: the motion itself has become wallpaper.
- **major:** one sentence shape used three or more times in the video ("So, X? Not quite.", "Think of a ...", a rhetorical
  question answered at once, a short dry fragment after every long sentence), or a shape the last two videos also leaned
  on. Quote each use.
- **minor:** the same strong move used so often it stops landing: more than one whip, more than about three punch-ins or
  music drops, a slam on every big number. Scarcity is what keeps a device working.
- **minor:** a metronomic rhythm: the *Devices* rhythm line shows most gaps within a quarter of the median, with no burst
  of quick changes and no deliberate long hold anywhere.
- **minor:** every joke built the same way (understatement, or the narrator's aside), or the same ending: the closing
  callback and the same hold every video uses.

For each major, propose one concrete alternative that fits THIS video's content, from the moves in `explain-concept`
*Break the formula* or one of your own, and say which line or picture it would carry. A finding with no alternative is
half a finding.

Say which other videos you read, in one line, before the findings.

**Done:** when you have named the formula moves this video repeats (with the earlier video each comes from), every
stretch of a minute or more with no pattern break, and every surprise that is decoration; and for each major, the
alternative.
